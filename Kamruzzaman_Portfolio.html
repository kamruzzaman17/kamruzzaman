Who I Am

Research Profile

I am an environmental scientist with a multidisciplinary background spanning soil biogeochemistry, ecotoxicology, and nutrient cycling, shaped by research experience across Bangladesh and Hungary.

My work focuses on understanding how soil ecosystems respond to environmental stressors — from nitrogen fertilisation and greenhouse gas dynamics to heavy metal contamination and microbial community interactions. I integrate classical soil analyses with molecular tools and quantitative statistical modelling to link ecosystem processes to real-world agricultural and environmental outcomes.

Currently, my MSc thesis at MATE investigates how soil organic matter modulates detoxification responses in Caenorhabditis elegans as a proxy for ecotoxicological risk assessment — an integrative approach that bridges molecular biology, soil chemistry, and environmental toxicology.

I am passionate about science that matters: research that informs sustainable agricultural policy, supports climate adaptation, and improves environmental quality for farming communities.

Nitrogen Cycling & Nutrient Management
Soil nitrogen supply estimation, NMI development, fertiliser optimisation, GHG emission dynamics, and nutrient balance modelling for diverse cropping systems.
Soil Biogeochemistry & Microbiology
Mycorrhizal fungi–soil interactions, microbial biomass and nutrient transformations, aggregate stability, and carbon cycling across land use types.
Ecotoxicology & Environmental Contamination
Heavy metal fate and remediation, fluoride contamination, soil organic matter–pollutant interactions, and organism-level toxicological responses.
Quantitative Modelling & Decision Support
Field-scale nutrient balance modelling, SEM, mixed models in R, and co-development of Android decision-support tools for farmers and researchers.
