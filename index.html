Who I Am

Research Profile

I am a soil scientist with training in ecotoxicology. My background spans soil biogeochemistry, nutrient cycling and contamination, built through research in Bangladesh and Hungary.

My work focuses on understanding how soil ecosystems respond to environmental stressors — from nitrogen fertilisation and greenhouse gas dynamics to heavy metal contamination and microbial community interactions. I integrate classical soil analyses with molecular tools and quantitative statistical modelling to link ecosystem processes to real-world agricultural and environmental outcomes.

My current work uses the LUCAS 2018 European soil survey and global literature data. I study how soil pH shapes enzyme ratios across Europe. I test whether routine laboratory data can predict soil carbon fractions in countries with no samples. I also ran a global meta-analysis on what drives permanganate-oxidizable carbon. All three manuscripts are now under review. Earlier, I developed the scientific models behind a field-validated nutrient balance calculator.
