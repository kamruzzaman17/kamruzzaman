Who I Am

Research Profile

I am an environmental scientist with a multidisciplinary background spanning soil biogeochemistry, ecotoxicology, and nutrient cycling, shaped by research experience across Bangladesh and Hungary.

My work focuses on understanding how soil ecosystems respond to environmental stressors — from nitrogen fertilisation and greenhouse gas dynamics to heavy metal contamination and microbial community interactions. I integrate classical soil analyses with molecular tools and quantitative statistical modelling to link ecosystem processes to real-world agricultural and environmental outcomes.

My current research programme uses the LUCAS 2018 European Soil Survey — spanning 28 countries and thousands of topsoil samples — to characterise how microbial metabolic demand and enzyme allocation between carbon and phosphorus acquisition vary across Woodland, Grassland, and Cropland systems at continental scale. Alongside this, I independently developed and published a field-validated nutrient balance calculator, and I continue to build statistically rigorous, reproducible pipelines that turn large environmental datasets into actionable insight for sustainable land management.

1

Microbial Metabolic Demand Co-varies with Enzyme Allocation Between Carbon and Phosphorus Acquisition in European Woodland and Cropland Soils

Soil Biology and Biochemistry In preparation · 2025 🧬 LUCAS 2018 Target: SBB

Using 420 topsoil samples from the LUCAS 2018 Soil Survey (Austria & Bulgaria), this study characterises how microbial metabolic demand (qO₂) co-varies with the allocation between carbon- and phosphorus-cycling enzymes across Woodland and Cropland systems. OLS path analysis identifies basal respiration (β = +0.362***) and soil pH (β = −0.122*) as independent drivers of qO₂, while qO₂ is negatively associated with β-glucosidase (partial ρ = −0.230*** in Woodland) and positively with acid phosphatase (β = +0.315***), confirmed by a significantly lower BG:AP ratio in Woodland soils (median 0.436 vs 0.702).

📊 n = 420 · R² = 0.699 · LUCAS 2018
2

Grassland and Woodland Soils Share a Common Microbial Physiological State Despite Contrasting Soil Chemistry: Evidence from the LUCAS 2018 Survey Across 28 European Countries

Global Change Biology In preparation · 2025 🌍 28 EU Countries Target: GCB

Using the LUCAS 2018 COMBINED dataset (n = 17,510 environmental; n = 683 microbial), this study demonstrates that Grassland and Woodland soils are physiologically indistinguishable in qO₂ (Wilcoxon p = 0.565 ns) and BG:AP ratio (p = 0.188 ns) despite significant differences in pH (1.5-unit contrast) and OC (1.6-fold contrast), while both land-use types differ strongly from Cropland (all p < 0.001). Agricultural management history, not soil chemistry, is the primary determinant of microbial physiological state at continental scale.

📊 n = 17,510 · 28 countries · LUCAS 2018
3

Nutrient Release or Salinity Risk? Mustard Oilcake Drives Progressive Macronutrient Mineralisation Without Ionic Penalty in Coastal Saline Soils of Bangladesh

Journal of Soil Science and Plant Nutrition In preparation · 2025 🌾 Saline Soils Target: JSSPN

This study investigates whether mustard oilcake application drives macronutrient (N, P, K) mineralisation in coastal saline soils of Bangladesh without exacerbating ionic stress. Findings characterise the temporal dynamics of nutrient release and evaluate the trade-off between organic amendment benefits and potential salinity penalty across soil depths and incubation periods.

🌾 Coastal saline soils · Bangladesh
